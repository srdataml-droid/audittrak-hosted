# AuditTrak MVP Walkthrough — speaker notes

## Slide 1

AuditTrak connects the records behind a job: what was agreed, billed, paid, delivered and confirmed. This is a local MVP, with fictional demonstration data.

## Slide 2

The first audience is an assumption to test with pilot users, not a validated market. Institutional reviewers are a second user role. Matching money alone cannot establish the commercial story.

## Slide 3

The product shows five stages: agreement, invoice, payment, fulfillment and confirmation. The second line groups invoice and payment only to keep this slide readable. Files and structured fields belong to the same event.

Amina has three stages present. Agreement, invoice and payment match, but delivery proof and current confirmation are missing, so the result remains Requires review. Coverage is not a calibrated probability or credit score. Source: docs/images/workspace.jpg.

## Slide 11

No message is sent automatically. Link possession is not independent identity verification. Reviewers add observations rather than credit decisions. All seeded reviewers can see all explicitly shared jobs in this local MVP; institutional grants are a production requirement.

## Slide 12

The evidence model includes business, counterparty, commercial event, agreement, invoice, transaction, fulfillment, attestation, evidence item, assessment, flags, profile and audit history. The Mono adapter exists but no live sandbox account was validated. AI proposals never overwrite records or deterministic rules.

## Slide 13

This is a local MVP, not a production financial system. A pilot must validate the audience hypothesis and operational safeguards. No traction, market size or revenue figures are claimed.
